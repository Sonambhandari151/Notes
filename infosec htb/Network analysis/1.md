
### **Network Traffic Analysis (NTA)**

- NTA is the examination of network traffic to:
    
    - Characterize common ports and protocols.
        
    - Establish a baseline for normal traffic.
        
    - Monitor, detect, and respond to threats.
        
    - Ensure insight into network behavior.
        
- Helps detect anomalies, security threats, and malware.
    
- Supports compliance with security guidelines.
    
- Essential as attackers often use legitimate credentials to evade detection.
    

---

### **Use Cases**

- Collect real-time traffic to detect upcoming threats.
    
- Establish baseline for normal network communications.
    
- Detect traffic from non-standard ports or suspicious hosts.
    
- Identify protocol issues (HTTP errors, TCP misconfigurations).
    
- Detect malware on the wire (ransomware, exploits).
    
- Investigate past incidents and support threat hunting.
    

---

### **Required Skills & Knowledge**

1. **TCP/IP Stack & OSI Model** – Understand network traffic and host application interactions.
    
2. **Basic Network Concepts** – Switching, routing, VLANs; differences in traffic depending on tap location.
    
3. **Common Ports & Protocols** – Quickly identify standard communication and potential anomalies.
    
4. **IP Packets & Sublayers** – TCP (stream-oriented) vs. UDP (connectionless) traffic understanding.
    
5. **Protocol Transport Encapsulation** – Dissect encapsulated data to analyze efficiently.
    
6. **Environment & Equipment** – Knowledge of tools and capture devices.

### **Common NTA Tools**

|Tool|Description|
|---|---|
|**tcpdump**|Command-line packet sniffer; uses LibPcap to capture/analyze traffic.|
|**TShark**|Command-line version of Wireshark; captures packets from live network or files.|
|**Wireshark**|GUI-based analyzer; in-depth packet/frame decoding.|
|**NGrep**|Packet-level grep; uses regex and BPF filters to analyze traffic.|
|**tcpick**|Sniffer focused on TCP stream reassembly.|
|**Network Taps**|Hardware to copy network traffic for analysis.|
|**SPAN Ports**|Switch/Router ports mirrored for traffic capture.|
|**Elastic Stack**|Ingest, analyze, and visualize traffic data.|
|**SIEMs**|Centralized logging, alerting, and analysis (e.g., Splunk).|

---

### **BPF Syntax**

- Berkeley Packet Filter (BPF) allows raw access to Data-Link layer.
    
- Used for filtering and decoding traffic in tools like tcpdump, NGrep, TShark.
- Berkeley Packet Filter (BPF) syntax is a language used for filtering network traffic, commonly employed by tools like `tcpdump` and Wireshark. It allows users to define specific criteria for selecting packets of interest.


---

### **Performing NTA**

- Can be simple (live console monitoring) or complex (capturing via tap and analyzing in SIEM).
    
- Capture placement is key: ensure visibility into the target network segment.
    
- Devices for capturing traffic: network taps, SPAN ports, port mirroring.
    

---

### **NTA Workflow**

1. **Ingest Traffic** – Capture network traffic; use filters if target known.
    
2. **Reduce Noise** – Filter out unnecessary traffic (broadcast, multicast).
    
3. **Analyze & Explore** – Examine hosts, protocols, TCP flags. Key questions:
    
    - Is traffic encrypted or plain text?
        
    - Are unauthorized access attempts occurring?
        
    - Are unusual host-to-host communications present?
        
4. **Detect & Alert** – Identify errors or potentially malicious traffic; use IDS/IPS for signatures.
    
5. **Fix & Monitor** – Implement fixes, then continue monitoring to ensure resolution.
