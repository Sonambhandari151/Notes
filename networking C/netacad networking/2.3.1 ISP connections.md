
## ISP SERVICES
- An ISP provides the link between the home network and the internet.
- An ISP can be the local cable provider, a landline telephone service provider , the cellular network that provides your smartphone services, or an independent provider who leases bandwidth on the physical network infrastructure of another company.
- Many ISP provides additional services like email accounts, network storage , and website hosting and automated backup or security services.
- It connects in a hierarchical manner ensuring that internet traffic generally takes the shortest path from the  source to the destination.
- Internet backbone is like an info super highway that provides high - speed data links to connect the various service provider networks in major metropolitan areas around the world.
- Primary medium that connects the internet backbone is **fiber-optic cable.** It is typically installed underground to connect cities within continents. It also run under the sea to connect continents, countries and cities.


## ISP connections
**Modem & Router:**

- The **modem** converts ISP signals into data your devices understand.
- The **router** distributes this connection via Wi-Fi or Ethernet.

for eg. the simplest ISP connection consists of a modem that provides a direct connection between a computer and the ISP. Not preferred as the computer is not protected on the internet.
- Most common connection: A router is required to securely connect a computer to an ISP. It consists of using a wireless integrated router to connect to the ISP. The router includes a switch to connect wired hosts and a wireless AP to connect wireless hosts.The router also provides client IP addressing info and security for inside hosts.

## Cable and DSL Connections

Most home network users do not connect to their service providers with fiber-optic cables.
Common methods for small offices and home users includes.

- **CABLE:** 
Cable internet comes from the same wire that brings cable TV to your home. This wire is called a **coaxial cable**.

Here’s how it works:

1. **The cable carries both TV and internet signals.**
2. **A special device called a cable modem** separates the internet signal from the TV signal.
3. **The modem connects to your router or computer** using an Ethernet cable.
4. **Your devices (phones, laptops, etc.) get internet** via Wi-Fi or wired connection.

- **DSL:** 
DSL (Digital Subscriber Line) uses your **telephone line** to provide internet without interrupting phone calls.

Here’s how it works:

1. **DSL runs on a regular phone line** but uses different channels for internet and calls.
2. **A DSL modem** separates internet signals from phone signals.
3. **The line has three channels:**
    - **Voice channel** (for calls 📞)
    - **Download channel** (for receiving data, faster)
    - **Upload channel** (for sending data, slower)
4. **Speed depends on the phone line quality** and **distance from the provider's office**—the farther you are, the slower it gets.

DSL is **always on**, but **not as fast** as fiber or cable internet. 


Other ISP connection options for home users includes:
## Cellular
**Cellular Internet** uses mobile networks (like 4G or 5G) to provide internet access.

### How it works:

1. Your phone or mobile hotspot **connects to the nearest cell tower** using radio signals.
2. The **tower sends and receives data** to keep you online.
3. **You can access the internet anywhere** with a cellular signal.

### Pros:

✔ Works in remote areas .
✔ Great for people who travel .

### Cons:

✖ Speed depends on **network strength** 
✖ **Data is limited** (extra charges for overuse )

It’s a **flexible** but **metered** way to stay connected! 

## Satellite
**Satellite Internet** is a good choice for areas where **DSL, cable, or fiber** are unavailable.

### How it works:

1. A **satellite dish** at your home **communicates with a satellite** in space. 
2. The satellite sends and receives data from a **provider's ground station** on Earth. 
3. Your **modem connects to the dish** and provides internet to your devices.

### Pros:

✔ **Works anywhere** (even in remote areas)   
✔ **Decent speeds** for browsing and streaming

### Cons:

✖ **Needs a clear sky view** (trees/buildings can block signals) 
✖ **Expensive setup** (dish + installation) 
✖ **Higher latency** (slower response times) 

It's a **lifeline for rural areas**, but **costly and slower** compared to wired connections. 

## Dial-up Telephone
**Dial-up Internet** is a **cheap but very slow** way to connect using a phone line.

### How it works:

1. A **modem dials** the ISP’s phone number .
2. The **internet connection is established** over the phone line.
3. **Only one can work at a time**—either internet or phone calls.

### Pros:

✔ **Low cost**   
✔ **Works anywhere with a phone line**

### Cons:

✖ **Very slow** (not good for large downloads) 
✖ **Disconnects if someone uses the phone** 
✖ **Outdated technology**

Use **dial-up** only if **no other internet options** are available! 

In metropolitan areas, many apartments and small offices are being connected directly with fiber-optic cables. This enables an internet service provider to provide higher bandwidth speeds and support more services such as internet, phone, and TV.

The choice of connection varies depending on geographical location and service provider availability.
