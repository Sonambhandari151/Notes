## LAN wireless Frequencies
Home networks commonly use **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz** unlicensed frequency bands.

- **Bluetooth** operates on **2.4 GHz**, enabling **low-speed, short-range, one-to-many communication** for peripherals like mice, keyboards, and audio devices.
- **Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11)** uses **both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz**, offering **higher power, longer range, and better throughput** than Bluetooth.

## Wired Network Technologies
While many home network devices use **wireless communication**, some applications benefit from a **wired switch connection** because it provides a **dedicated, stable, and high-speed link** without interference or sharing bandwidth with other users.

### **Wired Connections (Ethernet)**

- Devices are connected using **Ethernet patch cables** (usually **UTP** with **RJ-45** connectors).
- Some homes have **Ethernet jacks pre-installed** for easy wired networking.

### **Alternatives to Ethernet Wiring**

- **Powerline adapters** use **electrical wiring** to distribute network connections in homes without Ethernet cabling.

Wired connections are ideal for **gaming, streaming, and office work**, where **low latency and high reliability** are crucial.


WIRED TECHNOLOGIES
1. Category 5e Cable: Most common wiring used in LAN. Made up of 4 pairs of wires that are twisted to reduce electrical interference.
2. 2. Coaxial Cable: Coaxial cable has an inner wire surrounded by a tubular insulating layer, that is then surrounded by a tubular conducting shield. Most coax cables also have an external insulating sheath or jacket.
3. Fiber- Optic Cable:  Can be either glass or plastic with a diameter same as a human hair and it can carry digital information at very high speed over a long distance. Have a very high bandwidth , which enables them to carry very  large amounts of data.
